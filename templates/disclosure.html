Responsible disclosure — found something? Tell us straight

Sentinel Safeguard tests other people's systems for a living, with permission and in writing. This page is the same courtesy extended the other way: if you find a security problem in this website or in Sentinel's own systems, here is where it goes and what happens next.

Where it goes

Two doors, both answered

Use whichever fits the finding:

The report page

sentinelsafeguard.net/report-issue — the same page clients use, monitored directly by Sentinel. Pick the priority that honestly fits the issue.

Email

sentinel@sentinelsafeguard.net — Sentinel's own mail, on Sentinel's own server. The machine-readable version of this page lives at /.well-known/security.txt, in the standard format (RFC 9116) that security tools look for.

What helps

What to include

A clear description of the issue and what it could allow, the address or page where you found it, and the steps to reproduce it, if you have them. Screenshots or a short recording help. You do not need a polished report — a plain, specific note beats a formal, vague one.

You can expect an acknowledgment, and — if the issue is confirmed — a fix that is prioritized, with credit in the release notes for the fix, unless you would rather stay unnamed.

The lines

Good faith, drawn plainly

Research done in good faith — looking for problems, proving them with the smallest demonstration that works, and reporting them here — is welcome and will be met in the same spirit. The lines are the obvious ones: do not touch, change, download, or keep anyone else's data; do not run anything that degrades the site for other people; and stop and report once a problem is proven, instead of widening it. Those lines are what separate research from the thing Sentinel exists to stop.

Found a problem somewhere else — a system that is not Sentinel's? That is not a disclosure; that is the start of an engagement, and it runs authorization-first like everything else here: see the FAQ and How It Works.
