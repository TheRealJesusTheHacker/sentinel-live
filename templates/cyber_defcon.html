How this is set

The score is the sum of three public inputs. First, how many vulnerabilities CISA added to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog in the last 7 days (18 or more earns 3 points, 10–17 earns 2, 4–9 earns 1). Second, how many Threat Watch and Patch Alert items Sentinel News published in the last 7 days (45 or more earns 2 points, 25–44 earns 1). Third, how many attacking sources the DShield sensor network counted today (260,000 or more earns 2 points, 180,000 or more earns 1). The points add to a score of 0–7, which maps to the DEFCON scale like this:

  • Score 0–1 → DEFCON 5 — ROUTINE
  • Score 2–3 → DEFCON 4 — GUARDED
  • Score 4–5 → DEFCON 3 — ELEVATED
  • Score 6 → DEFCON 2 — HIGH
  • Score 7 → DEFCON 1 — SEVERE

There is no hand-tuning — the number is the number, and the inputs above are the exact values it came from. This level was formerly displayed as the “Sentinel Threat Level” on a 1-to-5 rising scale; the math is unchanged — only the name and the direction changed, to the DEFCON convention where the smaller number is the serious one.
