How an engagement works — first call to finished job

Most people who reach out to Sentinel have never hired a security company before, so the process is stated plainly: six steps, everything important in writing before work begins, and plain language the whole way through. Here is exactly how it goes.

Reach Out Talk & Look In Writing First The Work Report & Walkthrough After What We Ask

Step 01

Reach out

It starts with the contact form on this site or a call to 816-533-8418. Sentinel works by appointment only — no walk-ins, and no sales script on the other end either. You describe what you are building, what is breaking, or what keeps you up at night, and the conversation goes from there.

What to expect

A conversation, not a pitch: questions about your situation, honest first impressions, and — where the work sounds like a fit — a time set to look at it properly.

What it is not

Not a sales call. There is no script and no pressure, and a conversation carries no obligation — if the work is not a fit, Sentinel will tell you that straight, the same as it does on every other page of this site.

Step 02

Talk and look

Before anything is quoted, Sentinel looks at the actual environment. The scope conversation covers what you have, what worries you, and what "done" looks like for you — and on-site comes first, because a camera's blind spot and a network's weak point are found standing in the room, not guessed at from a desk.

How it happens

On site where the work is — your office, your property, your home — or by phone and screen where that fits the job better. Sentinel asks questions, looks at the systems involved, and takes notes; you ask anything you want answered.

What you do

Show the environment as it really is. A real scope is built on the honest picture — nothing in this step is a test you can fail, and nothing you show gets used for anything but the scope.

Step 03

In writing, signed before work

Nothing starts on a handshake and a hope. Before any work begins, you get it in writing: an Engagement Letter and a Scope of Work that spell out exactly what will be done, what it will cost, and what is not included — yours to read, question, and keep before you sign anything.

Two jobs carry one more signature. Penetration testing requires a signed Penetration Testing Authorization before any test begins — no system is ever tested without it. Work that handles evidence runs under a Chain of Custody, so where the evidence has been is documented at every step. How authorized testing works →

Why the paperwork matters

It protects you. The scope is the promise: if it is not in the Scope of Work, it does not happen and it does not get billed. Anything that changes is agreed and written down the same way, before it is done.

Your copy is yours

Everything you sign is yours to keep — the Engagement Letter, the Scope of Work, and every report that follows them. No portal you can be locked out of; no paperwork held back.

Step 04

The work

The work happens on the schedule agreed in the scope — arranged around your operation, not over it: a leasing office between residents, a restaurant between services, a shop before it opens. The disruption is planned down with the same care as the work itself.

While it happens

The work is documented as it goes — what was touched, what was changed, what was found — and you are kept informed in plain language as it progresses, not briefed afterwards in jargon.

If something changes

Findings sometimes change the picture. If the work uncovers something outside the signed scope, you hear about it before anything is done about it — the scope is only ever widened in writing, with your agreement.

Step 05

Report & walkthrough

When the work is done, you get the report — findings and fixes written in plain language — and a walkthrough of it, face to face where that fits: what was found, what it puts at risk, what was fixed, and what is worth doing next, in priority order. What held up is stated just as plainly as what did not.

What you walk away with

The written report, not just an invoice: the findings, the evidence behind them, and the fixes in the order they matter. For an install, you also get a walkthrough of the system itself — how to view it, manage it, and keep it yours.

No jargon tax

If a word in the report is not clear, the walkthrough is where it gets explained until it is. A report you cannot act on is not a deliverable.

Step 06

After

Delivery is not a disappearance. Questions about the report or the work are answered after it is delivered, and the report stays yours. Ongoing protection is available for the businesses and homes that want it — but it is always the client's choice, taken on its own merits, never a lock-in attached to a finished job.

If something breaks later

The report page is open around the clock — a report reaches Sentinel directly and starts the conversation, whether the problem is with finished work or something entirely new.

When you are ready for more

Many jobs start small on purpose. When the next step makes sense — the install after the assessment, the test after the hardening — it starts the same way this one did: a conversation and a scope in writing.

Emergencies

Something wrong right now?

An incident in progress skips the line. If an account is compromised, a machine is behaving like it is not yours, or data is somewhere it should not be, do not wait for the steps above — go straight to incident response, and use the report page, which is open around the clock and reaches Sentinel directly.

The partnership

What Sentinel asks of you

Three small things, asked respectfully — they are what let the work be done well and scoped honestly:

Access when it is scheduled. If a visit is set for a time, the systems and spaces in the scope need to be reachable then — a locked office and an absent keyholder turn one scoped visit into two.

One decision-maker. One person who can answer questions and approve changes keeps the work moving and the scope honest — especially when a finding needs a call in the middle of the job.

Straight answers about the environment. What is installed, who holds the passwords, what has already gone wrong. Sentinel works from the real picture; a scope can only cover what it can see.

That is the whole list. Everything else — the paperwork, the documentation, the plain language — is Sentinel's side of the bargain.
