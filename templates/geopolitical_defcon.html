How this estimate is set

Open-source estimate by Sentinel Safeguard. The actual DEFCON level is classified — this is not the official level. Five signal families are scored from public sources every hour. Family A, conflict intensity, counts geopolitical headlines carrying war, strike, invasion, ceasefire, and front-line language (5 or more earns 3 points, 3–4 earns 2, 1–2 earns 1). Family B, U.S. involvement and force posture, counts headlines about U.S. strikes, troops, carriers, deployments, NATO, and military aid (6 or more earns 3, 3–5 earns 2, 1–2 earns 1). Family C, nuclear rhetoric and doctrine, counts nuclear, warhead, ICBM, and ballistic-missile language (4 or more earns 3, 2–3 earns 2, 1 earns 1). Family D is the slow anchor: the Bulletin of the Atomic Scientists’ Doomsday Clock — at 90 seconds to midnight or less it earns 2 points, at 91–120 seconds 1 point. Family E, a GDELT conflict-volume trend, is opportunistic: when GDELT answers, a rising volume adds up to 2 points; when it does not, the estimate computes without it, and E can never count as an elevated family — a single-source volume metric informs pressure but cannot concur in a triangulation.

The family scores add to a pressure total. The candidate level follows fixed rules: DEFCON 1 requires an imminent-use signal, pressure of 9 or more, and at least three elevated families; DEFCON 2 requires a direct-confrontation signal, pressure of 7 or more, and at least three elevated families; DEFCON 3 requires pressure of 5 or more and at least two elevated families; pressure of 3 or more is DEFCON 4; anything quieter is DEFCON 5. A family is elevated at score 2 or more (for the clock, exactly its closest-ever band). That is the triangulation rule: no single feed can move this level alone — independent families have to concur.

The estimate also has inertia, because the real system dwells instead of jittering. A candidate level must repeat on consecutive hourly evaluations before it applies: two in a row for a move to DEFCON 3, 4, or 5, and three in a row for a move to DEFCON 2 or 1. A reading that reverts clears the pending move. Every applied move is written to the change log below with the exact inputs that drove it — the estimate scores itself in public.

Backtest: scored against history, this model puts the 1962 Cuban Missile Crisis peak at DEFCON 2, the 1973 Yom Kippur war at DEFCON 3 (pressure alone, with no confrontation flag, does not reach 2 — the actual 1973 move was to 3), September 11 at DEFCON 3, the post-2022 Ukraine era at DEFCON 3, and 1990s peacetime at DEFCON 5. Those anchors are pinned in the site’s tests, so a future change that breaks one fails the build instead of quietly rewriting history.
