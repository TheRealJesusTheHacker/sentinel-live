Who we protect — businesses and homes

Sentinel protects businesses and homes across the Kansas City metro. The blocks below are the common calls — the work Sentinel is built for — not a whitelist. If your world is not named here, the last block on this page is for you.

Focus 01

Apartment complexes & property management

An apartment community runs on systems residents never think about until one fails them: cameras over the lots and laundry rooms, resident portals, and a leasing office holding applications, IDs, and payment details — too often with the same password on everything. A property manager carries that across every site they run, so one weak office login can put every community's records in reach.

What Sentinel looks at first

The camera system — whether recorders are still on factory passwords, who can view the footage, and from where. Then the leasing office: account access, how resident files move, and whether the office network is separated from everything else.

The work it leads to

Camera and access installs done right the first time, office networks hardened site by site, and one number to call when a property has a problem.

Focus 02

Senior centers & care facilities

A senior center or care facility holds some of the most personal information in its community — resident records, family contacts, sensitive paperwork — much of it on front-desk computers that everybody uses, with cameras that families quietly count on. The people who live there are also the people phone and email scams target hardest, which makes the human side of this work as important as the systems.

What Sentinel looks at first

Who can get into the front-desk systems and what those systems hold; whether resident and family information is kept apart from everyday use; and whether the cameras actually record, get checked, and keep their footage.

The work it leads to

Hardened office systems, account access that makes sense, cameras that earn their place — and staff who know what a scam attempt looks like when one arrives.

Focus 03

Churches & nonprofits

A church runs a small business's worth of technology on a volunteer's spare time: donation platforms, membership and giving records, office email, livestream gear. There is rarely an IT person — which is exactly why weak passwords and forgotten accounts linger for years, and why a single compromised inbox can reach the whole congregation's trust.

What Sentinel looks at first

The donation and giving platforms, and who holds their logins; office email and records; the livestream and sound-booth machines that double as office computers; and the list of accounts nobody remembers creating.

The work it leads to

The basics done properly — accounts cleaned up, access sorted, systems hardened — in a form volunteers can actually keep up, without needing an IT department.

Focus 04

Local service companies & trades

For a tree service, an irrigation company, a contractor — the business lives in a phone and a customer list. Invoicing, scheduling, and years of customer names, addresses, and gate codes sit in apps and email that nobody has ever secured. Lose the phone, or the account behind it, and the season's work goes with it.

What Sentinel looks at first

The phone, and the accounts on it — backups, locks, and what happens the day it is lost on a job site. Then the customer list itself: where it lives, who can reach it, and whether the office computer is protected at all.

The work it leads to

Accounts and devices locked down, customer data backed up, and a plan for the lost-phone day written before that day happens.

Focus 05 — homes & families

Residential — homes & families

A home is a network now. The router, the doorbell camera, the smart TV, the kids' tablets, the work laptop on the kitchen table — a modern house runs more connected devices than a small office did a decade ago, and most of it was set up in an afternoon and never looked at again. Sentinel protects homes with the same seriousness it brings to businesses, because the scams and break-ins aimed at families come through the same wires.

What Sentinel looks at first

The router and the home Wi-Fi — who set them up, what is still on the defaults, and what is actually on the network. Then the cameras and smart devices: what they can see, what they report to, and which accounts stand in front of them.

The work it leads to

A hardened home network, cameras installed and configured properly, the device sprawl inventoried — and scam-proofing for the family's everyday tech: the calls and messages aimed at the people in the house, not just the machines.
