Privacy — what this site collects, and what it never does

Sentinel Safeguard is a security company, so this page is written the way Sentinel works: specifically, item by item, with nothing swept under “we may collect data.” The short version — nothing here is collected for advertising, nothing is sold, and the site’s one cookie is named and explained below.

The inventory

What this site collects, and why

Data reaches this site three ways: you send a form, you file a report, or you read a page. Here is each one, in full.

When you send the contact form

The form on the homepage and the Contact page collects your name, your email address, an optional subject, and your message. The time it was sent and the network (IP) address it came from are recorded with it — the address is how the site tells a person writing in from a machine flooding the form. Your message is stored in the site’s own database, on Sentinel’s own server, and the only way to read it is through the site’s gated admin side.

When you file a report

The Report an Issue page collects your name, your email address, an optional phone number, a category, the priority you pick, a subject, and your message — with the time and network address recorded the same way, into the same database behind the same gate. It exists so a problem you report can be worked and answered, and for nothing else.

When you simply read a page. The site keeps a visitor log of public page views. Each entry records the time, your network (IP) address, your browser’s user-agent string, the page you read, the page that referred you (when your browser sends one), and the country your connection appears to come from, as reported by Cloudflare. Entries from known bots and crawlers are marked as bots. The log is how Sentinel can see that the site is being read and where abuse is coming from. It covers public pages only — sending a form, calling the site’s data feeds, and the gated pages are not page views and are not entered in it.

Cookies

The one cookie

This site sets exactly one cookie of its own. It is named svid, and it holds a random number — a string of chance, not your name, not an account, nothing that identifies you by itself. Its one job is the visitor log above: the pages you read within the same half hour are grouped as a single visit, so Sentinel counts visits instead of raw page loads.

It is set with the HttpOnly, Secure, and SameSite=Lax flags, it is refreshed as you move between public pages, and it expires thirty minutes after your last one. Blocking it changes nothing about how the site works for you — every page loads the same; the log simply counts each page on its own. This site sets no other cookies of its own. Cloudflare’s bot check, on the form pages, may set cookies of its own — that is covered under Who else is involved.

Never

What we never do

No advertising trackers

Nothing on this site reports your visit to an ad network, and nothing here is built to follow you around the internet with ads.

No third-party analytics

There is no Google Analytics, no Meta pixel, and no outside analytics service on this site. The only counting is the first-party visitor log described above, on Sentinel’s own server.

Nothing is sold or shared for marketing

Your information is never sold, rented, or traded, and nothing you send through the forms is shared with anyone for marketing. Form messages are read through the gated admin side, to answer you — that is the whole list of readers.

The data pages are served from here

The threat, flight, and news data on this site is fetched by Sentinel’s server and served to you from here. Your browser does not fetch it from the data sources, and those sources are not told who you are.

Third parties

Who else is involved

Running a website on the open internet involves a few outside services. These are all of them, with the pages each one touches:

Cloudflare

Every request to this site passes through Cloudflare’s network — it carries the connection to Sentinel’s server, and it is the source of the country and connecting address recorded in the visitor log. On the form pages, Cloudflare’s Turnstile bot check runs in your browser to tell people from bots, and Cloudflare may set cookies of its own for that check. What Cloudflare sees, it handles under its own privacy policy.

Google Fonts

Every page loads Sentinel’s typefaces from Google Fonts (fonts.googleapis.com, with the font files themselves from fonts.gstatic.com). As with any web request, Google sees the network address the request came from.

Esri

The three map pages — Threat Map, Geopolitical, and Flights — draw their maps with tiles your browser loads from Esri’s ArcGIS servers (server.arcgisonline.com), for the area of the map on screen.

unpkg

The same three map pages load the Leaflet map library from the unpkg content delivery network.

Everything else — the pages, the forms, the data — is served from Sentinel’s own server. Links that point elsewhere, like GitHub or the Sentinel library, send nothing anywhere until you click them.

Retention

How long things are kept

Visitor log: 90 days

Visitor log entries are deleted automatically once they are 90 days old. Nothing in the log is kept longer.

Messages and reports: until deleted

There is no automatic deletion timer on a contact message or a report you send. It stays in the site’s database until Sentinel deletes it, because it is the record of your request — and if you want yours removed, the next section is the whole process.

One smaller note: the abuse-prevention counter behind the forms holds network addresses in memory only, for a few minutes at a time, and stores nothing.
