Kansas City · Cybersecurity

Cybersecurity for Kansas City small businesses

Most small businesses in the Kansas City metro run on technology nobody has ever sat down and secured. Sentinel starts by finding out where you actually stand, then closes the gaps in priority order — and gets you ready for the bad day before it arrives, not after.

Start with an assessment

An assessment is a plain look at what you have: the devices on your network, the accounts people sign in with, how the network itself is set up, and whether your backups and updates are real or assumed. Nothing is changed during an assessment — you get the findings first, in plain language, and decide what happens next.

Not sure where you stand right now? The free self-assessment covers the same ground in about three minutes, in your browser, with nothing sent anywhere. It is a starting point, not a verdict — the conversation comes after.

Hardening that holds

Where the assessment finds soft spots, Sentinel hardens them: secure configuration on the devices and accounts that matter, sensible access — people reach what their work needs and nothing more — and updates and backups that are verified rather than assumed. The work is scoped in writing before it begins, and documented when it is done, so you know what you have and how it is protected.

Who it is for

Small businesses and organizations across the Kansas City metro that hold customer data, take payments, or would struggle to explain a breach to the people who trust them — offices, service companies, and community organizations without an IT department of their own.

What you walk away with

Systems that are assessed, hardened, and documented — and a findings picture you can act on in priority order, written so a non-technical reader can follow every line of it.

Ready before the bad day

Breach readiness is the unglamorous work that decides how a bad day goes: current backups you have actually restored from, an account list you trust, and knowing what the first phone call is. Sentinel helps you put that groundwork in place ahead of time. If something is already wrong — a compromised account, a machine behaving strangely — that is incident work, and the order of operations matters: incident response on the Services page covers how Sentinel handles it.

This page is one part of the full picture. The Cybersecurity & IT service states the complete offering, and How It Works walks through how an engagement runs, start to finish.
