Questions — answered plainly

The questions a first call usually starts with, answered the way Sentinel answers them in person. Anything not covered here, the contact page is the place to ask it.

Do you take walk-ins?

No. Sentinel works by appointment only — there are no walk-in hours. Call 816-533-8418, email, or send the contact form, and a time is set. That is deliberate: the work is on-site first, at your business or home, not across a counter.

Where do you work?

The Kansas City metro. Sentinel is based in Kansas City, Missouri, and the service area is the metro around it — on both sides of the state line. If you are at the edge of the metro and unsure, ask; the answer is a conversation, not a fence. The full picture of who the work fits is on Who We Protect.

How does an engagement start?

With a conversation, then a look at your environment, then the scope in writing — signed before any work begins — then the work itself, a report and walkthrough of what was found, and support after. The whole flow, step by step, is on How It Works.

Do you really test whether you can break in?

Yes — that is penetration testing, and it is authorization-first, always. Nothing is tested without a signed Penetration Testing Authorization from the person who owns or is responsible for the systems, on top of the engagement paperwork. No signature, no test — no exceptions, no “just a quick look.” The authorization is what makes the work legal, scoped, and safe to run on systems a business depends on.

What does it cost?

There is no honest flat answer, so this page does not invent one. What drives the cost is the scope (what is being examined or built), the size (how many systems, people, and places), and the systems involved (what they are and what state they are in). Those are knowable after the first conversation and the look at your environment — and the price is put in writing, in the scope, before work begins. Nothing starts on a verbal estimate.

Something is wrong right now. What do I do?

Do not wait on a form. Go to the report page — it is open around the clock — or call 816-533-8418. If a machine is behaving like it is not yours, leave it on, unplug it from the network if you safely can, and change the passwords that matter from a different, clean device. How incident work runs is described under incident response.

Do you work with homes, or only businesses?

Both. Home networks, home offices, and the devices a household runs on are part of the work — see Who We Protect. The questions are the same at any size: what matters, where it lives, and what would hurt if it stopped or leaked.

I am not sure what I need. Is that a problem?

No — it is the most common starting point. “Everything, probably” is an honest answer. The free ten-question self-assessment turns it into a short list in about three minutes, entirely in your browser, and the first conversation starts from wherever you are.

What happens to what I send through this site?

Exactly what the privacy page says — item by item: what the forms collect, the one cookie, how long things are kept, and how to ask for a message you sent to be deleted. Sentinel is a security company; that page is written to be checked, not skimmed.

How do I report a security problem with this website itself?

Through the responsible disclosure page — it says where reports go, what to include, and what to expect back. Good-faith research is welcome; testing that touches anyone else's data is not.
