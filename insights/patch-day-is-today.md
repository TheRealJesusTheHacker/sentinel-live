## What happened

The federal government's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities list —
the must-patch list, kept by CISA — added serious flaws in
VMware's VeloCloud edge platform and the Collabora office suite
this month, and both were already being exploited when they
landed. That is the pattern now: a flaw is announced, and
working attacks follow within hours to days, not the weeks or
months people used to count on.

## Why it matters here

Most small networks do not run VeloCloud or Collabora, and this
note is not about those two products. It is about the clock.
Attackers now automate the hunt for unpatched systems the
moment a flaw is public, and they scan the whole internet to do
it — a business in the Kansas City metro is not too small to be
found, it is simply found by a machine instead of a person.
Every day an available update sits uninstalled is a day the
door stands open with a sign on it.

## What to do

- **Turn on automatic updates** everywhere it exists — phones,
  computers, browsers, and the apps on them. Let the machines
  patch themselves.
- **Update the edge first:** router, firewall, and VPN firmware
  before the things behind them.
- **Restart.** Many updates do not actually take hold until the
  device restarts; a machine that has been "about to restart"
  for a month is still unpatched.
- **Retire what no longer updates.** A device whose maker has
  stopped sending security updates is on borrowed time — plan
  its replacement before it becomes the way in.

**This week's version:** turn on automatic updates on every
device that offers it, and restart the machines that have been
putting it off. Patch day is not a monthly chore anymore — it
is today, and then it takes care of itself.
