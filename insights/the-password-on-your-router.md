## What happened

Reporting this month put the haul at roughly 70,000 sets of
credentials — usernames and passwords for the boxes that sit at
the edge of a network: firewalls, VPN gateways, and routers,
including equipment from major makers like Fortinet and Palo
Alto Networks. Nobody broke into 70,000 networks one at a time.
The logins were harvested from compromised computers and older
breaches, bundled up, and packaged for resale, the way anything
with a market gets packaged.

## Why it matters here

The edge box is the front door of a network, and it is the door
most often left on its factory settings. Small businesses and
homes across the Kansas City metro run routers and firewalls
that still carry the password they shipped with — one that is
printed in a manual anyone can download — or a password that
gets reused everywhere else. An attacker holding a bought list
does not pick your lock. They walk down the street trying keys,
and the doors that open are the ones whose keys were never
changed.

## What to do

- **Change the admin password** on your router, firewall, or
  VPN box to something long and unique — used for that box and
  nothing else.
- **Turn on multi-factor authentication** wherever the device
  offers it, especially on anything reachable from the internet.
- **Retire shared passwords.** If the same password opens the
  edge box and anything else, change both.
- **Update the firmware** while you are in there, and turn on
  automatic updates if the box offers them.

**The ten-minute version:** log in to your router or firewall
tonight, change the admin password to one you use nowhere else,
and check for a firmware update. That single change takes you
off the bulk list.
